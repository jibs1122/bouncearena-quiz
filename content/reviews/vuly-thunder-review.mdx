**Previous-generation model:** This review covers the superseded Vuly Thunder. New buyers should read our [current Vuly Thunder 2 review](/vuly-thunder-2-review) or [view Vuly's current range](/go/vuly). The details below remain relevant to existing owners and second-hand buyers of the original Thunder.

The Vuly Thunder uses leaf springs instead of coil springs, which removes the pinch and entrapment risk of a traditional trampoline. It has a 180 cm net and a self-closing entry. Assembly is a common owner complaint, and Vuly has acknowledged that the feet can sink into soft ground.

Vuly’s own guide outlines the differences between each model: [Choosing a Vuly Trampoline – Which Model Suits You?](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=blog/choosing-vuly-trampoline)

## Build quality & materials

The Thunder has a galvanised steel frame rated to 150 kg. The assembled weight (size L) is 122.2 kg.

However, it does not feature a powder-coated finish, which could have provided additional protection against the elements. Both the Thunder Pro and Springfree models have a powder-coated finish.

The leaf springs used on the Thunder in place of traditional springs eliminate common safety concerns relating to pinching and entrapment. Vuly covers them for 1 year, against 10 years on the frame.

The Thunder's small plastic feet can sink into soft ground, which leaves the frame unevenly supported. Vuly [has acknowledged the issue](/vuly-trampoline-assembly) on Thunder models, saying it happens when the trampoline sits on dirt or soft ground for a long time, and suggests putting something under each foot or shifting the trampoline a few centimetres every few months.

The warning label on the safety net states that it should be replaced every year.

## Safety features & standards

Besides the springless design, the Thunder has curved safety net poles and a 180 cm superfine mesh net. The net attaches directly to the mat and a skirt covers the leaf springs.

It meets both Australian and European safety standards, including AS 4989:2015, EN71-1:2014, EN71-2:2011+A1, and EN 71-14:2014.

### Important

One of the best features of the Thunder is its shade cover, which keeps leaves and other debris out while also protecting your children from the sun. Unfortunately, the shade cover isn't included in the basic package and [must be purchased separately](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/shade-cover).

## Bounce and performance

Without coil springs, the bounce feel differs, particularly for lighter users. Vuly says the leaf springs adjust to the jumper's weight.

## Ease of assembly

Assembly is a common complaint from owners, who say the instructions aren't much help. [Vuly’s assembly videos](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=installation) are a good place to start with the assembly process.

Many owners end up hiring a professional to install the Vuly Thunder. Note that Vuly also offers [their own installation service](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/trampolines/thunder).

## Value for money

The Thunder sold for above $2,000 in the Large size, though Vuly ran frequent sales. The current Thunder 2 starts at around $1,400.

## Additional features & accessories

The Thunder supports a range of optional accessories, including a shade cover, tent wall and basketball hoop. [You can also buy wheels](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/lift-pro-thunder-wheels) that allow you to raise and push your trampoline to a new location. While a ladder is not included with the Thunder, the frame itself provides easy access for older kids.

The entry is self-closing with no zip to break, and overlaps to keep jumpers inside. The included skirt stops children putting their hands through the leaf springs.

## Customer service

Customer service experiences are mixed, with some owners reporting difficulty resolving issues or obtaining replacement parts.

## Warranty

The Thunder's warranty is 10 years on the frame, 5 years on the jump mat, and 1 year on the safety net, net poles and leaf springs.

Note that Vuly does offer [an extended warranty](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=support-our-warranty) on the Thunder which extends the warranty period on the Safety Net, Poles, and Leaf Springs out to 3 years.

## Conclusion

The Vuly Thunder's strengths are its springless safety design and tall net. Its weaknesses are assembly, mixed customer service and feet that sink into soft ground.
