The Vuly Flare is Vuly's entry-level trampoline, with a galvanised steel frame. It keeps the safety design of Vuly's dearer models but has thinner padding, a lower weight limit and a shorter warranty.

Vuly’s own guide outlines the differences between each model: [Choosing a Vuly Trampoline – Which Model Suits You?](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=blog/choosing-vuly-trampoline)

![A Vuly Flare trampoline installed in a backyard](/images/posts/vuly-flare-3.jpg)

## Build quality & materials

The Vuly Flare has a galvanised steel frame but no powder-coated finish, which would add weather resistance.

The padding is thinner than on the [Vuly Ultra](/vuly-ultra-review), and Vuly backs the Flare's frame, springs and mat for 3 years against 5 on the Ultra.

Vuly lists a 100 kg maximum single-user weight for both Flare sizes, below the 120 to 150 kg per-jumper ratings on Vuly's other current models.

### Important

The Flare doesn't come with an anchor kit, so it isn't secured to the ground without the separate [Vuly Trampoline Anchor Kit](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/anchor-kit).

## Safety features & standards

The Vuly Flare meets the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015. It has curved safety net poles, springs outside the jumping area and a superfine mesh net. The mat and the safety net lock together, so jumpers can't reach the springs from inside.

## Bounce and performance

The Flare uses conventional coil springs to provide its bounce. Vuly does not publish a spring count or spring length for this model.

Owners generally describe the bounce as satisfactory. The 100 kg single-user limit and two-size range put it below Vuly's larger current models and suit it to younger children.

## Ease of assembly

Assembly is fairly straightforward, although owners say the instructions could be clearer. [Vuly’s assembly videos](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=installation) are a good place to start.

## Value for money

Priced from around $500, the Vuly Flare is Vuly's entry-level option. It suits families who aren't sure how much a trampoline will be used and don't want to spend much.

Compared with the [Vuly Ultra](/vuly-ultra-review), it has a shorter warranty, a lower weight limit and thinner padding, but it keeps the same curved poles, external springs and self-closing entry.

## Additional features & accessories

Optional accessories include a shade cover, tent wall and basketball hoop. Neither a ladder nor an anchor kit is included, which adds to the cost of a complete setup.

## Customer service

Owner reviews of Vuly's customer service are mixed, and some report late deliveries, especially around Christmas.

## Warranty

The Vuly Flare comes with a 3-year warranty on the frame and springs/mat, and a 1-year warranty on the safety net and pads. These terms are typical for trampolines in this price range.
