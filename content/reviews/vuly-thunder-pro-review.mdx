**Previous-generation model:** This review covers the superseded Vuly Thunder Pro, not the current Thunder 2 Pro. It remains useful for existing owners and second-hand buyers. If you are buying new, [view Vuly's current range](/go/vuly) or compare the [Thunder 2 Pro with the Ultra 2 Pro](/compare/vuly-ultra-2-pro-vs-thunder-2-pro/).

The Vuly Thunder Pro has a galvanised, powder-coated steel frame and weighs almost twice as much as the standard Thunder. Its leaf springs keep coil springs out of reach and its 190 cm net is the tallest of any model we've looked at. It is also expensive, heavy and hard to assemble.

Vuly’s own guide outlines the differences between each model: [Choosing a Vuly Trampoline – Which Model Suits You?](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=blog/choosing-vuly-trampoline)

### Build quality & materials

The Vuly Thunder Pro has a galvanised, powder-coated steel frame for corrosion resistance.

It weighs 217.7 kg assembled in the Large size, almost double the standard [Vuly Thunder](/vuly-thunder-review) at 122.2 kg and more than double a similar-sized [Springfree](/springfree-trampoline-review) at 96.6 kg. Vuly backs the frame for 10 years and the springs and mat for 5.

The weight has a downside. The feet sink into soft ground, which makes it hard to keep a lawn under it. Vuly [has acknowledged the issue](/vuly-trampoline-assembly) on Thunder models and suggests putting something under each foot or shifting the trampoline a few centimetres every few months. The Thunder Pro is better suited to harder surfaces.

### Important

One of the best features of the Thunder Pro is its shade cover, which keeps leaves and other debris out while also protecting your children from the sun. Unfortunately, the shade cover isn't included in the basic package and [must be purchased separately](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/shade-cover).

### Safety features & standards

The Vuly Thunder Pro has curved safety net poles and superfine mesh netting, and the mat joins to the net. At 190 cm, its safety net is the tallest of any model we’ve looked at.

Vuly’s leaf springs mean there are no coil springs for small hands to get trapped in and also means the frame is located away from the jumping area, and outside the safety net, further reducing the risk of injury.

The full-length frame combined with the skirt means that there is no underside access on the Thunder Pro, but it also makes it almost impossible to mow under.

The Thunder Pro complies with the Australian trampoline standard AS 4989:2015.

### Bounce and performance

The Thunder Pro uses leaf springs instead of coil springs. Springless trampolines are often assumed to bounce worse; Vuly says this system adjusts to different weights.

![Children using a Vuly Thunder Pro trampoline](/images/posts/vuly-thunder-pro-2.jpg)

### Ease of assembly

Assembly of the Thunder Pro is difficult, and owners describe the instructions as vague.

[Vuly's online tutorials](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=installation) help, and many owners hire a professional assembler. Vuly also offers [its own installation service](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/trampolines/thunder-pro).

### Value for money

The Thunder Pro sold in $2,400 to $3,600 territory depending on size, among the most expensive models on the market, though Vuly frequently ran heavy discounts. The current Thunder 2 Pro starts at around $2,100.

### Additional features & accessories

The Thunder Pro supports accessories including a shade cover, basketball hoop and tent wall.

The entry is full-length and self-closing with no zip, which is often a point of failure on older trampolines.

### Customer service

Customer service experiences with Vuly vary, with some customers reporting positive interactions and others facing challenges, especially when dealing with assembly or maintenance issues.

### Warranty

The warranty is 10 years on the frame and 5 years on the springs and mat. Safety nets and pads have a 1-year warranty, and Vuly recommends replacing the safety net yearly.

Note that Vuly does offer [an extended warranty](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=support-our-warranty) on the Thunder Pro which extends the warranty period on the Safety Net, Poles, and Leaf Springs out to 3 years.

The Vuly Thunder Pro suits families who put safety and durability first and can afford it. Its galvanised, powder-coated frame and leaf spring system keep coil springs and the frame away from jumpers.

Its weight means the feet sink into lawn, but it has the tallest safety net of any model we've looked at, a full-length frame and a self-closing entry.

Assembly is difficult and it is one of the most expensive trampolines on the market, though Vuly discounts it often.

The warranty is long, although replacing the net yearly adds to the cost of ownership.
