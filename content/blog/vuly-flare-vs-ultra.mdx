The Vuly Flare and Vuly Ultra are two of Vuly's coil-spring family trampolines, at different prices. The Ultra has thicker padding, a higher weight capacity and a longer warranty.

Vuly’s own guide outlines the key differences between each model: [Choosing a Vuly Trampoline – Which Model Suits You?](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=blog/choosing-vuly-trampoline)

**Update:** Vuly has replaced the Ultra with the [Ultra 2](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline/ultra-2) and [Ultra 2 Pro](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline/ultra-2-pro), and the Flare has been discontinued. You can [view Vuly's current trampolines](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline) or read our [Ultra 2 vs Thunder 2 comparison](/vuly-ultra-2-vs-thunder-2).

## Overview

### Vuly Flare

![A Vuly Flare trampoline installed in a backyard](/images/posts/vuly-flare-1.jpg)

[View at Vuly](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline/flare)

The Vuly Flare trampoline is the most affordable option in Vuly's range. It has coil springs and a safety net, and is aimed at families on a budget.

### Vuly Ultra

![A Vuly Ultra trampoline viewed from above](/images/posts/vuly-ultra-drone-shot.png)

[View at Vuly](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline/ultra)

The Vuly Ultra is a slightly more expensive model with a longer warranty period. It has thicker foam and PVC covering, a dual-layered spring system with smaller springs, and a higher weight capacity. The Ultra also comes in more sizes.

## Detailed comparison

### Build quality and materials

#### Vuly Flare

- **Frame:** Galvanised steel frame, though it lacks a powder-coated finish for extra durability.
- **Materials:** Thinner padding than the Ultra, and a 3-year warranty on the frame, springs and mat against the Ultra's 5.
- **Weight capacity:** 120 kg, indicating it’s primarily designed for children.

#### Vuly Ultra

- **Frame:** Galvanised steel but lacks a powder-coated finish.
- **Materials:** Thicker foam and PVC covering, with a dual-layered spring system.
- **Weight capacity:** 150 kg, suitable for both children and adults.

### Performance and bounce

#### Vuly Flare

- **Spring system:** Single-layered spring system with 70 springs, each 13.7 cm long.
- **Bounce quality:** Provides a decent bounce, suitable for small children.
- **User feedback:** Generally satisfactory, though some users note durability issues.

#### Vuly Ultra

- **Spring system:** Dual-layered spring system with 70 springs, each 13.7 cm long.
- **Bounce quality:** Deeper bounce, suitable for adults as well as children.
- **User feedback:** Owner reviews are positive about the bounce.

### Safety features

#### Vuly Flare

- **Safety net:** 185 cm high, with curved safety net poles.
- **Protection:** Mat and safety netting lock together to protect users from springs.
- **Standards:** Meets Australian Safety Standard AS 4989:2015.

#### Vuly Ultra

- **Safety net:** 175 cm high, also with curved safety net poles.
- **Protection:** Mat threads through the base of the net, preventing fingers from accessing the springs.
- **Standards:** Meets Australian Safety Standard AS 4989:2015.

### Anchor kit sold separately

Neither the Ultra nor the Flare comes with an anchor kit. Neither is particularly heavy, so the [Vuly Trampoline Anchor Kit](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=accessories/anchor-kit) is worth adding to secure the trampoline in high winds.

## Accessories

Both the Vuly Flare and Ultra can take a basketball hoop.

Both models used to offer a shade cover and a tent wall, but these have been discontinued for the Flare, so the Ultra is the only one of the two that takes them.

### Ease of assembly

#### Vuly Flare

- **Assembly process:** Fairly straightforward, though owners say some instructions aren't clear.

#### Vuly Ultra

- **Assembly process:** Generally easy, but some parts can be tricky to fit together correctly.
- **Instructions:** Owners report unclear instructions; [Vuly's assembly videos](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=installation) help.

### Value for money

#### Vuly Flare

- **Price (at time of publish):** around $550 for the Large.
- **Summary:** An affordable entry-level option, good for families unsure about long-term trampoline use.

#### Vuly Ultra

- **Price (at time of publish):** around $700 for the Large.
- **Summary:** Costs more, but has thicker padding, a higher weight capacity and a longer warranty.

## Which trampoline is right for you?

- **The Flare suits** families who want an affordable, entry-level trampoline for young children.
- **The Ultra suits** families who want thicker padding, a deeper bounce, a 150 kg weight capacity and a 5-year warranty on the frame, springs and mat, and are willing to pay more upfront. It is also the only one of the two that takes a shade cover.
