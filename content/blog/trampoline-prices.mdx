A backyard trampoline costs anywhere from under $300 to more than $8,000, and the extra money mostly buys a different spring system, a higher weight rating and a longer frame warranty.

## Under $700

This band is mostly coil-spring round trampolines from Kmart, Jump Star, Kahuna and Plum, plus the entry models from Vuly and GeeTramp. On the 10 ft and larger models, weight ratings run from 100 kg to 150 kg for a single jumper. Frame warranties are mostly 1 to 5 years or not stated at all; GeeTramp's 10 years on the Curve is the exception.

The Flare and the Curve are the two in this band listed as meeting [AS 4989:2015](https://www.standards.org.au/standards-catalogue/standard-details?designation=as-4989-2015), the Australian trampoline standard. The Flare's 3-year frame warranty is the shortest in Vuly's range. Kmart's listing gives no warranty period for the frame, mat or net.

## $700 to $1,500

The core models from Vuly, Jumpflex and Oz Trampolines sit here. Frame warranties step up to 5 or 10 years and mat warranties to between 1 and 5 years. The Vuly Thunder 2, at the top of the band, is the cheapest model that keeps its springs below the mat; it uses leaf springs under the mat instead of coil springs around it, and is covered by a 10-year frame warranty. The Lifespan HyperJump 3 is a cheaper [springless](/springless-trampolines/) option, but its elastic straps sit at mat height.

A from-price is for the smallest size in the range; the larger Vuly sizes cost more. Oz Trampolines' figure is the RRP, and the [deals page](/trampoline-deals-sales/) shows it on sale for much of the year, so the price you pay is often lower. The Ultra 2's 5-year frame warranty is half what the HERO and Thunder 2 offer.

The Jumpflex MEGA's 225 kg rating is the highest of any model in this guide, which matters if adults will be jumping. GeeTramp's figure is for the cheapest bounce configuration; the higher-bounce versions cost more. [Mr Trampoline](/brands/mr-trampoline/)'s 9 x 15 ft family rectangles are the most expensive here at more than $8,000, with a 30-year frame warranty and 8 years on the mat.

## What the extra money buys

**Spring system.** Coil springs are standard below the Thunder 2's price. The exceptions are the elastic-band springless models from Kmart, Kahuna and Lifespan Kids, none of which is listed as meeting the standard, and Springfree's 1.9 m Mini Round, the only fibreglass-rod model under $1,500 and rated for 80 kg.

**Weight rating.** Under $700 the limit on a full-size model is 100 kg to 150 kg per jumper. The mid band reaches 160 kg with Jumpflex, and only the MEGA goes past 200 kg. Springfree is 80 kg on its Mini and Compact sizes and 100 kg on everything larger.

**Warranty.** The frame warranty moves from unstated or 1 to 5 years under $700, to 5 or 10 years in the mid band, to 10 years or more above $1,500. Mat and net cover is where the top brands differ: 10 years each at Springfree, 5 and 1 on Vuly's Pro and Thunder models and the Jumpflex HERO and MEGA, 3 and 2 at GeeTramp.

**AS 4989:2015.** Vuly, Springfree, Oz Trampolines and GeeTramp list every current model as meeting the standard, and ACON lists some sizes. Jumpflex, Kmart, Jump Star, Kahuna and BERG do not confirm it on their listings.
