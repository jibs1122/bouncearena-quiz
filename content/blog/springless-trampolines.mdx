This guide compares three springless trampolines sold in Australia: the Springfree and the two Vuly Thunder models.

![A Springfree trampoline part-way through assembly](/images/posts/springfree-trampoline-3.png)

## Vuly Thunder

The Vuly Thunder is the cheapest of the three. It shares most of the safety features and build of the Thunder Pro.

Vuly’s blog has a guide to the differences between its models: [Choosing a Vuly Trampoline – Which Model Suits You?](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=blog/choosing-vuly-trampoline)

![A child jumping on a Vuly Thunder trampoline](/images/posts/vuly-thunder-1.jpg)

[View at Vuly](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline/thunder)

### Key features

- **Springless design**: Leaf springs, which Vuly says give a smooth, consistent bounce.
- **Safety net**: 180 cm tall.
- **Frame**: Galvanised steel.
- **Weight capacity**: Supports up to 150 kg.
- **Self-closing entry door**: Doesn't run the full height of the safety net.

### Pros and cons

**Pros**:

- 10-year frame warranty.
- Self-closing entry.
- Lowest price of the three.

**Cons**:

- Assembly can be difficult.
- Feet can sink into soft ground.
- Lacks powder-coated frame.

### Conclusion

The Vuly Thunder suits families who want a springless design at the lowest price of the three.

## Vuly Thunder Pro

The Vuly Thunder Pro adds full-length leaf springs, a taller net, a powder-coated frame and a full-length door over the standard Thunder.

![Children using a Vuly Thunder Pro trampoline](/images/posts/vuly-thunder-pro-2.jpg)

[View at Vuly](https://www.vulyplay.com/aff/100/?url=trampoline/thunder-pro)

### Key features

- **Springless design**: Full-length leaf springs that stretch from the bouncing surface to the ground, giving a deeper bounce than the standard Thunder.
- **Safety net**: 190 cm tall.
- **Frame**: Galvanised, powder-coated steel for rust resistance.
- **Weight capacity**: Can support up to 150 kg.
- **Full-length entry door**: Extends the full height of the safety net for easier access.

### Pros and cons

**Pros**:

- Powder-coated frame.
- Full-length leaf springs for deeper bounce.
- High safety net.
- No underside access.
- Full-length door.

**Cons**:

- High cost.
- Complex assembly.
- Heavy, which can affect lawn maintenance.
- Feet can sink into soft ground.

### Conclusion

The Vuly Thunder Pro suits families who want the taller net, sealed underside and powder-coated frame and can pay the higher price.

## Springfree Trampoline

Springfree trampolines use fibreglass rods instead of metal springs. The rods and frame sit under the mat, so there are no springs or frame edge to land on.

![A Springfree trampoline part-way through assembly](/images/posts/springfree-trampoline-3.png)

[View at Springfree](/go/springfree)

### Key features

- **Springless design**: Uses fibreglass rods instead of metal springs.
- **Safety net**: Curved poles and fine mesh netting.
- **Frame**: Galvanised, powder-coated steel.
- **Maximum single-user weight**: 100 kg on the full-size Springfree models. The separate 700 kg figure is a static test rating, not a user capacity.

### Pros and cons

**Pros**:

- Springless design and curved net poles.
- Clearly published single-user and static test ratings.
- Durable construction.

**Cons**:

- High initial cost.
- Assembly can be complex.
- Zip on the enclosure can wear out over time.

### Conclusion

Springfree suits families prioritising safety and durability over price.

## Final thoughts

- **Springfree Trampoline**: Best for families prioritising safety and durability; 10-year warranty on the frame, rods, mat and net.
