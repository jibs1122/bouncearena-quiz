Both brands sell rectangular trampolines aimed at more serious use, and both provide 10-year frame warranties on their main lines. Every [GeeTramp](/brands/geetramp/) model is listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015, while no [Jumpflex](/brands/jumpflex/) model is. Jumpflex also offers square models, while GeeTramp offers in-ground versions of both the Curve and Force.

GeeTramp's Curve and Force models provide a 2-year net warranty against 1 year from Jumpflex, though Jumpflex provides a 5-year mat warranty on the HERO and MEGA compared with 3 years on GeeTramp.

## Quick verdict

GeeTramp suits anyone who wants a model that meets the Australian standard, an in-ground option, or a longer net warranty. Jumpflex could be the better fit if you want a square mat or a longer mat warranty.

## Full spec comparison

Jumpflex uses coil springs on the round models and piano wire springs on the square and rectangular MEGA. GeeTramp uses coil springs in round and rectangular, above ground or in-ground.
