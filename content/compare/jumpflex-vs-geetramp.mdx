Both brands sell rectangular trampolines aimed at more serious use, and both provide 10-year frame warranties on their main lines. Every GeeTramp model is listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015, and no Jumpflex model is.

Jumpflex has the higher capacity. The MEGA is rated to 225 kg per jumper and 450 kg combined, where GeeTramp's Force tops out at 150 kg per jumper. Jumpflex also offers square models, which GeeTramp does not.

GeeTramp's above-ground Curve and Force models provide a 2-year net warranty against 1 year from Jumpflex, though Jumpflex provides a 5-year mat warranty on the HERO and MEGA against 3 years on GeeTramp. GeeTramp also offers in-ground versions of both the Curve and Force, which list no net warranty. In-ground installs remove the climb and suit yards where a raised trampoline would dominate.

## Quick verdict

GeeTramp suits anyone who wants a model that meets the Australian standard, an in-ground option, or a longer net warranty on an above-ground model. Jumpflex is the better fit if you need the higher weight rating, particularly for adults using a MEGA, or want a square mat.

## Full spec comparison

Jumpflex uses coil springs on the round models and piano wire springs on the square and rectangular MEGA. GeeTramp uses coil springs in round and rectangular, above ground or in-ground.
