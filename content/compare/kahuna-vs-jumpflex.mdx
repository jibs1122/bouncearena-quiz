Neither of these brands has a model listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015, so if the Australian standard is a firm requirement, neither is the answer. What separates them is the length of their warranties and how much weight they will take.

Kahuna is the cheaper brand at every size and lists a general one-year Australian warranty without separate frame, mat and net terms. Jumpflex costs more and publishes 10-year frame warranties on the HERO and MEGA and 5 years on the FLEX, with 5-year mat warranties on the HERO and MEGA.

Capacity is the other gap. Jumpflex rates the MEGA to 225 kg per jumper and 450 kg combined, and the HERO to 160 kg, against 150 kg on Kahuna's largest models. Both brands make rectangular models; Jumpflex also makes square models, while Kahuna adds oval models and the round Twister Springless.

Choose Kahuna if the budget is fixed and you want the largest trampoline you can get for it, or if you want an oval or lower-priced springless option. Choose Jumpflex if you want clearly published longer frame coverage or a higher weight rating for older kids and adults.

## Quick verdict

Kahuna offers more low-priced size, shape and spring-system choices, but publishes only a general one-year warranty. Jumpflex costs noticeably more and publishes up to a decade of frame coverage, making it the clearer choice when long component coverage matters.

## Full spec comparison

Kahuna is mostly coil-sprung across round, oval and rectangular shapes, with a springless elastic-band Twister line. Jumpflex uses coil springs on the round FLEX and HERO and piano wire springs on the square and rectangular MEGA. The warranty and weight rows are where the price difference shows.
