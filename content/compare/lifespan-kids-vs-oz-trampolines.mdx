These two ranges overlap heavily on price. Lifespan Kids has the springless HyperJump 3, which replaces steel springs with elastic straps, and backs its frame for 10 years. Oz Trampolines is coil-sprung throughout, and every current Summit and OzUltimate model is listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015. No Lifespan model is.

Published warranty on the other parts generally favours Oz: most current models list 2 years on both the mat and net, against 1 to 2 years on the Lifespan mat and 1 year on the net. Oz rates its larger models to 150 kg per jumper against 140 kg at the top of the Lifespan range, and offers oval shapes for narrow yards. Both brands also offer rectangular models.

## Quick verdict

Oz Trampolines suits a family that wants a model listed as meeting the Australian standard and longer warranties on the wear parts. Lifespan Kids makes sense if a springless mat or the 10-year frame warranty is the priority, or if you want springless without paying Vuly or Springfree prices.

## Full spec comparison

Lifespan Kids covers round and rectangular across elastic-strap and coil-spring models, with frame warranties that vary by line. Oz has a consistent 5-year frame warranty.
