Every model from both brands is listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015, but they differ on shape and installation.

Vuly is round only, in five model families running from the entry-level Flare to the leaf-spring Thunder 2 Pro. GeeTramp makes the round Curve and the rectangular Force, and offers both as in-ground models. A rectangle spreads bounce more evenly across the mat, which suits older kids and anyone practising tricks; an in-ground install removes the climb and lowers the visual impact in the yard.

GeeTramp provides a 10-year frame warranty across the range and a 2-year net warranty on its above-ground Curve and Force models; its in-ground models do not include a net or list a net warranty. Vuly reaches 10 years on the frame only for its Ultra 2 Pro and Thunder models and provides a 1-year net warranty. Vuly provides a mat warranty of up to 5 years against 3 years on GeeTramp. GeeTramp's prices go higher at the top of its range, and both rate their best models to 150 kg per jumper.

## Quick verdict

For a round above-ground trampoline, Vuly has the broader choice, and it suits anyone who wants the leaf-spring option or a lower entry price. GeeTramp is the better fit if you want a rectangle, an in-ground install, or a 10-year frame warranty on a mid-priced model.

## Full spec comparison

Vuly's range is split between coil and leaf springs, and GeeTramp uses coil springs throughout.
