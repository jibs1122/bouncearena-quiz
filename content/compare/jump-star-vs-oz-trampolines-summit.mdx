These two ranges line up almost exactly on size and spring count, from 8 ft up to 16 ft, using the same kind of coil-spring construction. The differences are which models are listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015, warranty and price.

Every Summit Round size is listed as meeting AS 4989:2015. No Jump Star model is. The Summit also comes with a 5-year frame warranty against 3 years from Jump Star, plus 2-year mat and net warranties against 1 year each, and publishes combined weight limits where Jump Star does not.

Jump Star is cheaper at matching sizes. Its larger models are rated to 150 kg per jumper against 110 to 130 kg on the Summit, so at the top of the range Jump Star carries more weight for less money.

Choose the Summit if you want a model that meets the standard and longer warranties on every part. Choose Jump Star if the price is the deciding factor or you want the higher weight rating at the large sizes.

## Quick verdict

The Summit meets the standard, its warranties are longer, and it publishes combined weight limits. Jump Star is cheaper, and its biggest models carry a higher per-jumper rating.

## Full spec comparison

Both are round, coil-sprung, with enclosures and near-identical spring counts. Compare the Australian standard row and the three warranty rows, which all favour the Summit.
