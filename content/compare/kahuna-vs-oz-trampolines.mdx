These two ranges overlap at the budget end, and both sell most of their models below four figures. Oz uses coil springs in round, oval and rectangular shapes. Kahuna is mostly coil-sprung across those shapes but also includes the round Twister Springless. The Australian standard and published warranty detail separate them.

Every current Oz Trampolines model, across both the Summit and OzUltimate ranges, is listed as meeting AS 4989:2015. No Kahuna model is listed as meeting the Australian standard. Oz publishes a 5-year frame warranty and lists 2-year mat and net warranties on most current models. Kahuna lists a general one-year Australian warranty but does not break out separate frame, mat and net terms.

Kahuna is cheaper, particularly at the smaller sizes, and its range starts lower than anything Oz sells. Oz rates its larger models to 150 kg per jumper, matching Kahuna's largest, and publishes combined weight limits where Kahuna does not.

Choose Oz Trampolines if you want a model that meets the standard and more detailed component warranties for a little more money. Choose Kahuna if the lowest possible price or its springless option is the deciding factor.

## Quick verdict

Every current Oz Trampolines model is listed as meeting the standard, component warranties are published on most models, and combined weight limits are provided. Kahuna wins on entry price and adds a springless option. For buyers who want that published detail, Oz is the more straightforward choice.

## Full spec comparison

Oz uses coil springs in round, oval and rectangular shapes. Kahuna covers the same shapes, mostly with coil springs, and adds the round Twister Springless. The Australian standard applies to every current Oz model; compare the component warranty rows, which are not separately published for Kahuna.
