GeeTramp and ACON both offer a 10 × 17 ft rectangle. The differences are price, warranty terms, and which models are listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015.

Every GeeTramp model is listed as meeting AS 4989:2015. Three of ACON's five models are. GeeTramp is also substantially cheaper, starting under a thousand dollars where ACON starts well above that, and its range includes in-ground versions of both the round Curve and the rectangular Force.

ACON goes higher on price and spring count, reaching 140 springs on the 16 HD. Its 750 kg figure is a static frame test rating, not a combined user limit. GeeTramp rates its Force to 150 kg per jumper against 120 kg on the ACON X; ACON does not publish a single-user limit for the 16 HD. Both come with 10-year frame warranties. ACON offers 5 years on the mat against 3 from GeeTramp, while GeeTramp's above-ground models offer 2 years on the net against 1 from ACON; the in-ground GeeTramp models have no net.

## Quick verdict

GeeTramp suits anyone who wants a rectangle that meets the Australian standard or an in-ground install without ACON pricing. ACON is the better fit if you want its build, large 10 × 17 ft rectangles, the highest spring counts or the longer mat warranty.

## Full spec comparison

Both are coil-sprung in round and rectangular. Neither brand publishes a combined user limit.
