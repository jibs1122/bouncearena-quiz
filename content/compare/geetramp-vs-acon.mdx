GeeTramp and ACON both build for performance rather than for the cheapest possible backyard setup, and both offer a 10 × 17 ft rectangle. The differences are price, warranty terms, and which models are listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015.

Every GeeTramp model is listed as meeting AS 4989:2015. Three of ACON's five models are. GeeTramp is also substantially cheaper, starting under a thousand dollars where ACON starts well above that, and its range includes in-ground versions of both the round Curve and the rectangular Force.

ACON goes higher on price and spring count, reaching 140 springs on the 16 HD. Its 750 kg figure is a static frame test rating, not a combined user limit. GeeTramp rates its Force to 150 kg per jumper against 120 kg on the ACON X; ACON does not publish a single-user limit for the 16 HD. Both come with 10-year frame warranties. ACON offers 5 years on the mat against 3 from GeeTramp, while GeeTramp's above-ground models offer 2 years on the net against 1 from ACON; the in-ground GeeTramp models have no net.

Choose GeeTramp if you want a rectangle that meets the standard or an in-ground install without ACON pricing. Choose ACON if you want its premium build, large 10 × 17 ft rectangles or the highest spring counts.

## Quick verdict

GeeTramp covers most of what buyers come to ACON for at a lower price, and it is listed as meeting the standard across the whole range. Choose ACON for its high spring counts and longer mat warranty, not on the assumption that its 750 kg static frame rating is a combined user limit.

## Full spec comparison

Both are coil-sprung in round and rectangular. GeeTramp adds in-ground versions of each. Compare the Australian standard, maximum single-user weight, mat warranty and net warranty rows. Neither brand publishes a combined user limit.
