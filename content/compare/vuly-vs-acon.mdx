ACON starts at roughly the price of Vuly's best model and climbs from there. The two brands are competing for different buyers, but they overlap at the top of the Vuly range.

Every Vuly model is listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015. Three of ACON's five models are. Vuly is round only, with coil-spring models and the leaf-spring Thunder 2 and Thunder 2 Pro, which move the springs away from the jumping area. ACON is coil-sprung in round and one 10 × 17 ft rectangle, with high spring counts aimed at a stronger bounce.

On capacity, Vuly rates its Ultra 2 Pro and Thunder models to 150 kg per jumper against 110 to 120 kg on ACON's round models. ACON does not publish a comparable weight rating for the rectangular 16 HD. Both provide 10-year frame warranties at the top of their ranges; ACON provides a 5-year mat warranty and Vuly up to 5 years, with 1-year net warranties from both.

Choose Vuly if you want every model listed as meeting the standard, a leaf-spring option, or a considerably lower price. Choose ACON if you want a rectangle or the highest spring counts.

## Quick verdict

Vuly covers the majority of Australian backyards at a fraction of ACON's cost, and every model is listed as meeting the Australian standard. ACON is a specialist purchase worth considering for a large rectangle or high spring count, but its 750 kg static frame rating should not be read as a combined user limit.
