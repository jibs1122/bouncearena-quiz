Every model from both brands is listed as meeting the Australian Trampoline Standard AS 4989:2015. Springfree has no springs: flexible fibreglass rods sit under the mat, keeping steel away from the jumping surface entirely. GeeTramp uses conventional coil springs, in round Curve models and rectangular Force models, each available above ground or in-ground.

GeeTramp starts at around a third of Springfree's entry price and rates its Force to 150 kg per jumper against 100 kg on full-size Springfree models. Springfree provides 10-year frame, mat and net warranties; GeeTramp matches the frame warranty, provides 3 years on the mat, and lists 2 years on the net for its above-ground Curve and Force models. The in-ground GeeTramp models have no net or net warranty. Springfree also goes larger at the top of its range and offers square models.

## Quick verdict

Springfree suits anyone who wants the springless design and 10-year warranties on every part. GeeTramp suits anyone who wants a rectangle, an in-ground install, a higher weight rating or a much lower price, with the trade-off that it is a conventional sprung trampoline.
